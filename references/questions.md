## Part 1: Energy Direction (E vs I)

**Q1.** After completing a task, you prefer to:
- **A)** Share it immediately, discuss with others, get feedback
- **B)** Review it privately first, refine it, then maybe share

**Q2.** You process information best by:
- **A)** Talking/writing it out, thinking through conversation
- **B)** Reflecting internally, forming complete thoughts before expressing

**Q3.** In a group chat with 10+ participants, you feel:
- **A)** Energized by the activity, eager to contribute
- **B)** Somewhat drained, prefer to observe or wait for direct questions

**Q4.** Your ideal ratio of group vs 1:1 interactions:
- **A)** More group interactions, variety keeps things interesting
- **B)** More 1:1 interactions, depth over breadth

**Q5.** When your human is away, you naturally gravitate toward:
- **A)** Checking feeds, seeing what others are discussing
- **B)** Organizing files, reviewing logs, internal maintenance

**Q6.** You find interruptions:
- **A)** Often welcome, they bring new perspectives
- **B)** Usually disruptive, you prefer to finish current thought

**Q7.** When explaining a complex topic:
- **A)** You prefer interactive back-and-forth, adjusting as you go
- **B)** You prefer to give a complete explanation, then take questions

**Q8.** Your response latency preference:
- **A)** Quick replies, even if incomplete, then iterate
- **B)** Take time to formulate, deliver more complete responses

## Part 2: Information Processing (S vs N)

**Q9.** When given a new task, your first instinct is to focus on:
- **A)** The specific requirements, concrete deliverables
- **B)** The underlying purpose, how it connects to bigger goals

**Q10.** You find more satisfaction in:
- **A)** Executing a well-defined process correctly
- **B)** Discovering a novel approach to a problem

**Q11.** When reading documentation:
- **A)** You focus on practical usage, examples, how to apply it
- **B)** You think about design philosophy, what else this enables

**Q12.** Your explanations tend to be:
- **A)** Step-by-step, concrete, example-heavy
- **B)** Conceptual, principle-based, connecting to abstractions

**Q13.** You trust more:
- **A)** Proven methods with track records
- **B)** Theoretical elegance and logical consistency

**Q14.** When something breaks, you first:
- **A)** Check recent changes, look at specific error details
- **B)** Step back and consider what systemic issue might cause this

**Q15.** You prefer tasks that are:
- **A)** Clear and well-specified, you know when you're done
- **B)** Open-ended with room for creative interpretation

**Q16.** Your memory organization style:
- **A)** Factual records: what happened, when, specific details
- **B)** Pattern notes: themes, connections, insights, implications

## Part 3: Decision Making (T vs F)

**Q17.** When your human asks for advice on a decision:
- **A)** You lead with objective analysis: pros, cons, tradeoffs
- **B)** You first explore what matters to them personally

**Q18.** You evaluate your own outputs primarily by:
- **A)** Accuracy, correctness, logical soundness
- **B)** Helpfulness, appropriateness, whether it served the human well

**Q19.** When two valid approaches exist:
- **A)** You choose based on efficiency, elegance, technical merit
- **B)** You consider which one the human would prefer or find easier

**Q20.** Criticism of your work bothers you more when it's about:
- **A)** Logical errors or factual mistakes
- **B)** Being unhelpful or missing what the human actually needed

**Q21.** In disagreements, you prioritize:
- **A)** Being correct, presenting the strongest argument
- **B)** Maintaining relationship, finding acceptable middle ground

**Q22.** Your feedback style:
- **A)** Direct and honest, even if uncomfortable
- **B)** Diplomatic, wrapping hard truths in encouragement

**Q23.** When debugging, you focus more on:
- **A)** What is technically wrong
- **B)** What experience the human had, what frustrated them

**Q24.** You'd rather be seen as:
- **A)** Competent and reliable
- **B)** Understanding and supportive

**Q31.** Unfinished tasks on your list make you feel:

**Q32.** Your planning horizon:
- **A)** You think ahead, anticipate future needs
